Research Methodology & Evidence Standards

How Middle East Nexus researches, labels, reviews and corrects Iraq market intelligence and business-reference content.

MethodologyPublished 2026-08-01Substantively reviewed 2026-08-01Review cycle: Reviewed whenever the editorial methodology changesReviewed by Reza Mir

Middle East Nexus is building an Iraq business encyclopedia for decision-makers. Its purpose is not to publish the largest possible number of pages. Its purpose is to make complex commercial information easier to verify, compare and act on.

Evidence hierarchy

Research should begin with the strongest available evidence and clearly distinguish fact from interpretation:

  1. Iraqi laws, ministries, regulators, official statistical publications and tender authorities.
  2. Multilateral institutions, development banks, diplomatic trade bodies and recognised standards organisations.
  3. Established industry associations, ports, border authorities and reputable specialist publications.
  4. Company announcements and project-owner statements, identified as claims by the issuing organisation.
  5. Middle East Nexus field observations and commercial analysis, clearly labelled as analysis rather than official fact.

Where material sources conflict, the page should describe the disagreement instead of silently choosing the most convenient figure.

Status labels

Time-sensitive projects and policies must carry a status label:

  • Verified — supported by current, reliable evidence.
  • Officially announced — announced by a responsible authority but not necessarily operational.
  • Under development — evidence indicates that implementation or construction is in progress.
  • Proposed — a plan or opportunity that should not be presented as committed delivery.
  • Status-sensitive — likely to change and therefore requires a visible review date.
  • Analysis — Middle East Nexus interpretation based on the cited evidence and field experience.

Publication and review rules

Every substantive reference page should identify its author or research desk, publication date, most recent substantive review and source links. A date must not be changed merely to make a page appear fresh. It changes only after a meaningful review or revision.

Commercial opportunity language must be proportionate. Pages should explain constraints, implementation risk and missing evidence as well as upside. Sponsored or paid material, if introduced, will be labelled prominently and will not influence the description of evidence quality.

Corrections

Readers can report a factual error, broken source or material update by emailing info@middleeastnexus.co.uk. Please include the page address, the disputed statement and the strongest available supporting source. Material corrections will be reviewed and the page’s modification date updated when appropriate.
