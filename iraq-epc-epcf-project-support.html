Decision support

Test whether the project has the ingredients required for serious engagement

The labels EPC and EPCF do not prove that a project is ready. A credible opportunity requires an identifiable employer or project owner, authority to proceed, defined scope, land and approvals, a procurement or investment route, realistic payment or financing logic and parties capable of carrying their obligations.

Project authority

Identify the owner, beneficiary, public authority, investment commission, landholder and party empowered to negotiate or contract.

Land and approvals

Clarify land allocation or title claims, planning status, investment licences, environmental requirements, permits and unresolved dependencies.

Scope maturity

Assess concept design, output specifications, site information, demand assumptions, bills of quantity, interfaces and missing technical studies.

Financing logic

Examine who is expected to finance, the proposed security and repayment source, sovereign or bank support claims and conditions precedent.

Consortium capability

Map the engineering, construction, technology, supply, operations, finance and Iraqi execution roles—and the evidence each member must provide.

Delivery and payment risk

Flag currency, guarantees, tax, customs, escalation, variations, delay, security, local-content and dispute-resolution questions for specialist review.

A controlled process

How the engagement is structured

The exact scope is agreed before work begins. Each stage is designed to expose weak assumptions early and keep decisions tied to verifiable facts.

Clear boundaries

What this service does not promise

Middle East Nexus provides commercial intelligence, coordination and practical market-access support. The following boundaries protect both the client and the quality of the engagement.

  • EPC or EPCF labelling is not treated as evidence of funding, sovereign guarantee, land availability, procurement authority or contract award.
  • Middle East Nexus does not guarantee financing and does not provide regulated investment, lending or securities services.
  • Engineering design, feasibility certification, legal due diligence and financial modelling require appropriately qualified advisers under separate scopes.
  • No consortium member or Iraqi counterpart is presented as approved until each party completes its own governance and due-diligence process.

Common questions

Iraq EPC and EPCF project questions

What is the difference between EPC and EPCF support?

EPC focuses on engineering, procurement and construction delivery. EPCF adds an expected financing component, which introduces additional requirements around project economics, security, repayment, lender diligence and financial regulation. The proposed structure must be evidenced rather than inferred from the label.

Can Middle East Nexus bring an international consortium?

We can help define required capabilities and coordinate discussions with suitable engineering, investment and infrastructure parties where there is a credible project fit. Participation remains subject to each party's independent review, approvals and diligence.

Can you verify a government guarantee?

We can request and organise documentary evidence and identify the authority that would need to issue or approve support. Legal validity, enforceability and financial value must be assessed by qualified legal and financial advisers.

What documents are needed for an initial project screen?

Useful inputs include ownership or mandate documents, land evidence, investment or sector approvals, concept design, capacity and demand assumptions, cost estimate, proposed procurement and financing route, project schedule and correspondence with relevant authorities.

Do you represent the project owner or the contractor?

The client and mandate must be explicit. We do not act for both sides on the same decision without transparent conflict management and written agreement.
