From Tehran to Baghdad: A Tale of Building Amidst the Ruins (Part II)

2003 — Baghdad · Reza Mir, Founder

With the $30,000 investment I received from my sponsor, I was able to equip the shop I had purchased. I made an agreement with an Iraqi partner to manage the store. According to our arrangement, without contributing to the initial capital, he would receive half of the store's profits.

I was the only foreigner working in Baghdad's market, which was both a source of hope and a cause for concern. Foreigners were easy targets for terrorists, and another significant threat was the frequent kidnappings carried out by insurgent groups demanding ransom.

However, instead of worrying about my own life, I was more concerned about my sponsor's capital. Anticipating the risk of being kidnapped, I gave my Iraqi partner written instructions that under no circumstances should any ransom be paid for my release, as the money did not belong to me.

The team I built performed exceptionally well during the first year. With an initial investment of just $5,000 for the shop and $30,000 in funding, we managed to generate nearly half a million dollars in sales.

Iraqis, having recently emerged from the oppressive walls of a ruthless dictatorship, had little familiarity with the outside world. This became a significant advantage for our team, as our store quickly became a central hub for fulfilling a variety of commercial demands.

This incredible success made all the hardships and life-threatening challenges of living in Baghdad feel more bearable.
