Middle East Nexus Market-Entry View

Baghdad is strongest for companies able to combine technical delivery with disciplined stakeholder mapping. Before committing resources, verify the sponsoring authority, land ownership, investment licence pathway, bid or negotiation status, payment mechanism, local-content expectations, security plan and dispute-resolution structure.

Best-fit company profiles

EPC and EPC+F contractors; water and wastewater specialists; healthcare developers; mixed-use and housing developers; food-processing and cold-chain operators; engineering consultants; project-finance and PPP advisers.

First diligence questions

Is the opportunity open today? Who owns the land? Which authority signs? Is sovereign or bank-backed payment contemplated? Are feasibility studies complete? Is the project investment-law, public-contract or PPP based?

Key execution risk

Confusing an official sector listing with a live procurement. Several official pages aggregate opportunities from different publication periods, so written status confirmation is essential.

Practical next step

Prepare a one-page capability statement tied to a specific project category, then seek written clarification from the NIC, Baghdad Investment Commission and responsible ministry before submitting technical or financial material.

Operating Environment

FCDO reference: All but essential travel (most of province); all travel to Sadr City & areas south of Airport RoadReviewed: Jul 2026Not security advice

Baghdad is the federal capital and the centre of government, finance and international business, with the highest concentration of institutional counterparts and security infrastructure in the country. Official UK FCDO advice designates most of the province as "all but essential travel," with stricter "all travel" advice for Sadr City and areas south of the Airport Road. Routes to and from the airport can close at short notice, and political gatherings and large crowds should be avoided. In practice, most international commercial activity operates through managed security arrangements, vetted local partners and controlled movement. Companies should budget for professional security, secure accommodation and transport, and build route-closure contingency into project planning.

Important: This is a commercial-planning summary drawn from public official sources, not a security assessment or guarantee. Security conditions in Iraq change quickly and vary within provinces. Any company must obtain a current, professional security risk assessment for its specific sites, routes and personnel, and monitor official travel advice (e.g. UK FCDO, US State Department) before deploying staff or committing to operations.

Middle East Nexus Market-Entry View

Basra rewards companies that can combine heavy-industry capability with strong logistics planning, local stakeholder coordination and realistic environmental and utility assumptions. Marine access, customs, industrial land, water availability, grid connection and interface with state-owned companies are often as important as the core technology.

Best-fit company profiles

Port and marine contractors; EPC and EPC+F firms; desalination and water-treatment specialists; petrochemical and fertiliser technology providers; pipeline contractors; logistics operators; industrial rehabilitation specialists; healthcare and housing developers.

Legacy opportunity listings, unclear interface responsibility, feedstock and utility assumptions, environmental approvals, port-access constraints, payment security and delays in multi-agency decisions.

Practical next step

Prepare a Basra-specific capability note that links technical credentials to one defined opportunity theme, then request written status confirmation from the NIC, Basra Investment Commission, General Company for Ports of Iraq or the relevant state company.

Maysan (Amara) Investment & Opportunity Brief

Commercial Position

Maysan, with its capital Amara on the Tigris, is one of Iraq's principal oil-producing provinces and home to the Missan Oil Company (Iraq's second-largest producer at times). Its strongest themes are hydrocarbons and refining, water and infrastructure, vertical housing, mining and industry, and a large proposed border economic city. The province has a strong security record and an explicitly investor-friendly provincial government.

Editorial rule: Maysan shows a healthy mix of delivered projects (water plants, oilfield facilities, mining) and very large proposals (a reported US$65bn border economic city). Delivered and proposed items are clearly separated; headline proposals require confirmation of feasibility and financing.

Middle East Nexus Market-Entry View

Maysan suits hydrocarbons, water, infrastructure, mining and housing investors, supported by a strong provincial security record and an unusually welcoming local government. The province mixes bankable delivered projects with very large proposals, so disciplined separation of the two is key.

Best-fit company profiles

Oilfield services and refining EPC/supply firms; water and sanitation specialists; bridge and infrastructure contractors; mining and minerals investors; vertical-housing developers; industrial and manufacturing investors.

First diligence questions

Is the border economic city at concept, feasibility or financing stage? Which refinery packages are open? Is a housing scheme provincial or investment-law based, and is land title clear?

Key execution risk

Over-weighting a very large proposal (e.g. the US$65bn city) before feasibility and financing are demonstrated. Anchor decisions on delivered and financed workstreams.

Practical next step

Engage the Maysan Investment Commission (miciq.com) and, for oil and water, the responsible companies and ministries, with a capability statement tied to a named opportunity.
