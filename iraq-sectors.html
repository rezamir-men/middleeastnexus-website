Why sector-based market entry?

Most companies do not begin their Iraq journey by choosing a province — they begin with what they do. An engineering firm thinks in terms of water or power; a contractor thinks in housing and infrastructure; a supplier thinks in oil and gas. Organising Iraq by sector meets companies where they are, and answers the first question any board asks: is there a real, funded market here for what we offer?

Each sector guide gives the national picture — market size, the projects that are actually moving, who awards the work, how contracts are structured, and where the barriers sit. That is the strategic layer: it tells you whether Iraq is worth your time, and where the demand concentrates.

But a sector view alone is not enough to win work. Iraq is federal and intensely local; the same industry behaves very differently in Basra, Erbil or Nineveh. That is why every sector connects to our governorate briefs, which carry the local detail — the counterparts, the conditions and the practicalities on the ground. Read together, the two layers move a company from "is there a market" to "which province, which project, which partner" — the questions that actually decide whether an entry succeeds. It is also how a market this complex becomes navigable rather than overwhelming.
