Executive summary

The opportunity in brief

The market
Iraq has a large public health system, a growing urban private market and continuing demand for new hospitals, primary-care facilities, equipment, medicines, diagnostics, digital systems and trained staff.

Market overview

A health system moving from recovery to expansion

Iraq's healthcare market is not one uniform system. Federal institutions, governorate health directorates, the Kurdistan Region, public hospitals and a sizeable private market all operate with different decision-makers and procurement routes. WHO's evaluation of its contribution in Iraq reported 295 public-sector hospitals and 155 registered private-sector hospitals, with private facilities concentrated mainly in larger cities.

The direction of travel is expansion and modernisation. In a joint statement in April 2025, Iraq's Ministry of Health and WHO reported the opening of 15 new public hospitals, 116 primary-health-care centres and health houses, and 82 popular medical clinics. At the same time, gaps remain in specialist capacity, rural access, maintenance, health information, diagnostics and workforce deployment.

Healthcare data in Iraq is fragmented and reported through several systems. This page uses official or multilateral sources and labels older baseline data clearly rather than presenting it as current.

Demand drivers

What is creating sustained demand

Population and urban growth

Large and expanding urban centres require more beds, clinics, diagnostics and emergency capacity.

New hospital capacity

New public and private facilities require design, fit-out, equipment, commissioning and long-term maintenance.

Noncommunicable disease

Cancer, cardiovascular disease, diabetes and chronic respiratory conditions increase demand for diagnostics and long-term care.

Technology gaps

WHO has identified limited availability of advanced imaging, laboratory investigation and radiotherapy in parts of the system.

Digital transformation

DHIS2, digital civil registration and electronic health systems create opportunities in software, integration, cybersecurity and training.

Private-sector growth

Urban private hospitals, clinics, laboratories and pharmacies form a significant commercial channel alongside public procurement.

Buyers & decision-makers

Who awards and influences the work

  • The federal Ministry of Health and its technical, planning, procurement and private-sector departments.
  • Governorate directorates of health, which manage public hospitals and local health infrastructure.
  • Public teaching hospitals, specialist centres and medical-city institutions.
  • The National Investment Commission and provincial investment commissions for investment-licence projects.
  • Private hospital owners, diagnostic groups, laboratories, pharmacy chains and pharmaceutical distributors.
  • The Kurdistan Regional Government's Ministry of Health and regional investment authorities.
  • International organisations and development partners for selected health-system, primary-care and humanitarian programmes.
Before approaching the market, determine whether the opportunity is a federal procurement, governorate purchase, hospital-level requirement, investment licence, private transaction or Kurdistan Region project.
Procurement & market-entry routes

How companies enter the market

Public tenders

Supply, works and service tenders issued by the Ministry of Health, directorates of health, hospitals and other public entities.

Hospital EPC and fit-out

Design, construction, clinical planning, equipment packages and commissioning for new or rehabilitated facilities.

Local distributor or agent

A common route for devices, consumables, pharmaceuticals and laboratory products requiring local registration and support.

Private hospital sales

Direct commercial sales and service agreements with private hospitals, laboratories and diagnostic centres.

Investment licence

Private hospital, specialist-centre, pharmaceutical or manufacturing projects through national or provincial investment commissions.

Operations and maintenance

Long-term equipment maintenance, biomedical engineering, facilities management, clinical training and hospital operations.

Market-entry risks

What to resolve before committing

  • Medical-device, pharmaceutical and importer-registration requirements must be checked product by product.
  • Tender specifications may require local representation, bid bonds, performance guarantees, references and Arabic documentation.
  • After-sales service, spare parts, calibration, consumables and user training can be as important as the initial sale.
  • Federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region have distinct authorities and administrative processes.
  • Payment terms, budget release, letters of credit and currency exposure require project-specific due diligence.
  • Counterparty verification and anti-bribery, sanctions and beneficial-ownership checks should be completed before engagement.
  • Healthcare statistics are not always current or harmonised, so project-level verification is essential.

FAQ

Common questions

Who buys healthcare equipment and services in Iraq?

The main public buyers are the Ministry of Health, governorate directorates of health, public hospitals and specialised centres. Private hospitals, laboratories, pharmacies, distributors and the Kurdistan Region form separate routes to market.

What are the strongest healthcare opportunities?

Hospital delivery, medical equipment, imaging, diagnostics, laboratories, pharmaceuticals, digital health, maintenance, training, oncology, rehabilitation, maternal and child health and emergency systems.

Can foreign companies supply medical devices?

Yes, but product registration, importer arrangements, tender eligibility, documentation, after-sales service, training and spare-parts support must be resolved before bidding or shipment.

Is the private market important?

Yes. WHO has documented a substantial urban private sector made up of hospitals, clinics, laboratories, pharmacies and diagnostic centres, although public provision remains essential, particularly outside major cities.

Is a local partner required?

Not for every structure, but a capable local distributor, agent, investor or service partner is often commercially necessary for registration, tenders, logistics, installation, maintenance and relationship management.

How is the Kurdistan Region different?

It has its own health ministry, investment authorities and administrative processes. Companies should decide early whether they are entering Federal Iraq, the Kurdistan Region, or both.
