How to Enter the Iraqi Market: A Practical Guide for International Investors

Originally published February 2025 · Updated July 2026 · Reza Mir, Founder

Iraq is one of the Middle East's most significant — and most misunderstood — investment destinations. While headlines focus on security risks, sophisticated investors are quietly establishing positions in one of the world's largest construction booms. Here is what you need to know.

The Legal Framework

Iraq's Investment Law provides substantial incentives for foreign investors: a 10-year tax holiday, import duty waivers on essential materials, and the ability to repatriate capital and profits. A 2009 amendment permits foreigners to own land for housing projects, with 50-year leases available in other sectors.

The Reality on the Ground

Success in Iraq depends on relationships, not contracts. Iraqi business culture places enormous weight on trust, personal connections and a demonstrated long-term commitment. Courts are ill-equipped to resolve commercial disputes — which is why choosing the right local partner is the single most important decision an international investor will make.

Security and Operations

The security situation has improved significantly since 2017. Commercial operations in Baghdad and the southern provinces proceed normally for established operators. Violence does not typically target international businesses, and most experienced operators work without incident.
