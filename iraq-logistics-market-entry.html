Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about logistics and entering the Iraqi market.

What is the best way to move goods into Iraq?

Most cargo enters Iraq through Umm Qasr port in the south, the Kurdistan border crossings from Turkey, or by air. The right route depends on the goods, the destination governorate, customs treatment and timelines. A landed-cost view — freight, customs, clearance and inland transport combined — matters more than any single leg.

Do I need a local partner to operate in Iraq?

Not always. A foreign company can register a branch without a local partner. However, for government contracting and certain regulated sectors, a joint venture with an Iraqi partner is often advisable — and sometimes effectively required — because it provides pre-qualification status and existing relationships.

What are the main risks of trading in Iraq?

The most common challenges are customs delays, documentation and legalisation requirements, payment and banking compliance, security logistics in certain areas, and identifying reliable counterparties. Most are manageable with local knowledge, correct paperwork prepared in advance, and proper due diligence on partners.

How does Middle East Nexus support market entry?

We begin with the commercial objective, then map the market, relevant stakeholders and suitable counterparties, and handle practical operating requirements — from customs clearance and inland logistics to local coordination. Scope ranges from a focused briefing to ongoing on-the-ground support.
