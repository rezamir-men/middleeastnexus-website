China and Iraq: How Eastern Capital Is Reshaping Reconstruction

December 2024 · Reza Mir, Founder

Chinese investment in Iraqi infrastructure has accelerated dramatically since 2017. From the $27 billion TotalEnergies energy deal (in which Chinese state entities hold significant stakes) to construction contracts across southern Iraq, eastern capital is becoming the defining force in Iraq's reconstruction economy.

The Pattern of Chinese Engagement

Chinese contractors and investors operate differently in Iraq than their Western counterparts. They move quickly, tolerate higher risk, and prioritise long-term market positioning over short-term returns. The Boland Payeh company — which holds the contract for the Tajiat Olympic Stadium — exemplifies the model of Iranian and regional contractors backed by Chinese financing.

The Avik International Partnership

Middle East Nexus has formalised a strategic partnership with Avik International, Beijing — three Chinese investors committed to 100% financial backing of our Iraq infrastructure and development projects. This partnership is not symbolic: it provides the capital backing that allows us to bid for and execute projects at a scale that was previously impossible for a UK-registered independent operator.

What This Means for Our Clients

For international companies and investors seeking to work in Iraq, our Chinese partnership provides something rare: a single counterparty that combines Western governance and local Iraqi expertise with Eastern capital. This is the combination the market requires.
