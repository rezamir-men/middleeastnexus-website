Iraq Border Trade Corridors

A commercial view of Iraq's twelve border governorates, six neighbouring countries and the onward routes that connect Iraqi businesses and international investors to Europe, the Gulf, the Red Sea, Central Asia and the Mediterranean.

Strategic routes

Six corridors, different commercial roles

A shared border does not automatically create a high-capacity trade route. The commercial value of each corridor depends on the operating status of the crossing, customs treatment, road capacity, security, insurance, cargo restrictions and access to ports or third-country markets.

Türkiye Europe

Northern logistics corridor

Duhok's Ibrahim Khalil gateway anchors road freight between Iraq and Türkiye. The strongest opportunities are bonded and non-bonded warehousing, cold chain, fleet support, customs services, cargo visibility and onward access to Mediterranean ports.

Beyond the border: Mersin, İskenderun, Europe, Black Sea and Caucasus markets.
Iran Central Asia

Eastern multi-gateway corridor

Seven Iraqi governorates touch Iran, creating several routes for food, agriculture, construction materials, industrial inputs, oilfield supplies, passenger movement and regional distribution.

Beyond the border: Iranian Gulf ports, Tehran, the Caspian, Caucasus, Central Asia and Afghanistan.
Jordan Red Sea

Trebil–Aqaba corridor

Anbar provides Iraq's principal road connection to Jordan and the Port of Aqaba. The route supports imports, exports and transit cargo, with opportunities in dry ports, consolidation, truck services, storage and customs-compliant through movements.

Beyond the border: Aqaba, Red Sea shipping, Egypt and global maritime routes.
Saudi Arabia GCC

Arar and Jumaima corridors

Arar in Anbar is operational for passenger and trade movements. Muthanna's Jumaima road is a development corridor. Najaf's geographic border has strategic potential but should not be presented as a major active freight gateway.

Beyond the border: Saudi markets, GCC road networks and Red Sea ports.
Kuwait Gulf

Safwan–Abdali corridor

Basra carries the established commercial route to Kuwait. It connects Iraqi demand with Kuwaiti suppliers, ports and distribution networks and can form part of longer Türkiye–Iraq–Kuwait TIR movements.

Beyond the border: Kuwait ports, GCC markets and Gulf shipping.
Syria Mediterranean

Western and north-western corridor

Anbar, Nineveh and Duhok meet Syria. Al-Qaim, Al-Waleed and Rabia have different operating and security conditions; Fishkhabur has a special regional status. Every shipment requires a current route check.

Beyond the border: Syrian markets, the Levant and potentially Mediterranean ports.

Investment lens

Where private operators can create value

Warehousing and consolidation

Bonded storage, cross-docking, inventory control, secure yards and inland-container services near high-volume crossings.

Cold chain

Temperature-controlled transport and storage for food, pharmaceuticals and specialist agricultural exports.

Fleet and driver services

Truck parking, maintenance, tyres, fuel management, weighing, driver facilities and emergency support.

Customs and compliance

Documentation, ASYCUDA support, cargo visibility, TIR processes, inspection coordination and shipment-level due diligence.

Border hospitality

Hotels, food, passenger transport, medical support and seasonal capacity for pilgrimage and high-volume travel periods.

Industrial and logistics parks

Serviced land for packaging, light manufacturing, repair, distribution and value-added processing near strategic corridors.

Verify operating status

Crossings can close, reopen or change permitted cargo with little notice. Confirm with the responsible authority and carrier before dispatch.

Separate geography from access

A governorate may touch a country without having a high-capacity commercial crossing. Do not market a potential route as operational.

Check the full route

Customs, sanctions, insurance, security, axle limits, driver visas and third-country transit rules must be assessed end to end.
