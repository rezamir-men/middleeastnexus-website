Decision support

Reduce the risk hidden behind a promising introduction

A well-connected individual is not automatically a capable distributor, contractor or project partner. The right assessment depends on the role the partner must perform, the evidence available and the risks created by ownership, authority, reputation, sanctions exposure, financial capacity and delivery history.

Capability fit

Test whether the counterparty has the technical staff, licences, facilities, geographic reach and operational resources required for the proposed role.

Corporate identity

Confirm the legal name, registration details, stated ownership, authorised representatives and related entities as far as accessible records allow.

Track record

Seek project references, customer evidence, delivery examples and the counterpart's exact role rather than relying on broad claims.

Reputation and conflicts

Review adverse information, disputes, political exposure, competing interests and relationships that may create commercial or compliance risk.

Sanctions and integrity

Flag names and entities requiring formal sanctions, anti-bribery, export-control or enhanced compliance review by qualified providers.

Commercial alignment

Assess expectations on exclusivity, commission, territory, payment, staffing, reporting, introductions and responsibility for local execution.

A controlled process

How the engagement is structured

The exact scope is agreed before work begins. Each stage is designed to expose weak assumptions early and keep decisions tied to verifiable facts.

01

Role specification

Define what the partner must do, minimum evidence, exclusions, geography and the client's compliance requirements.

02

Longlist research

Identify candidates from sector networks, official records, market evidence and client-supplied names.

03

Desktop screening

Review identity, ownership indicators, activities, references, adverse information and obvious capability gaps.

04

Local verification

Where scoped, conduct interviews, reference calls, document requests or site observations and record what could not be confirmed.

05

Shortlist and controls

Rank fit, present red flags and recommend deeper legal, financial, sanctions or technical checks before appointment.

Clear boundaries

What this service does not promise

Middle East Nexus provides commercial intelligence, coordination and practical market-access support. The following boundaries protect both the client and the quality of the engagement.

  • No screening process can eliminate fraud, concealment or future misconduct, especially where records are incomplete or not independently accessible.
  • The service does not provide a legal opinion, audit, credit rating, sanctions clearance or forensic investigation unless separately delivered by qualified specialists.
  • A positive commercial assessment is not a guarantee of performance, solvency, authority or regulatory approval.
  • Middle East Nexus does not accept undisclosed commissions from candidates in a client-funded selection mandate.

Common questions

Partner identification and Iraqi due diligence

Can you verify whether someone is genuinely connected to a ministry or project owner?

We can examine the claim, request evidence, map the relevant institution and seek appropriate confirmation where possible. Informal access is not treated as proof of authority, procurement influence or a mandate to act.

What is the difference between partner identification and due diligence?

Identification searches for candidates against defined criteria. Due diligence examines specific candidates and their claims, risks and evidence. A proper mandate may use both stages, but they should not be confused.

Can you check beneficial ownership?

We review accessible corporate records and request ownership information, but availability and reliability vary. Complex structures or high-risk cases may require enhanced corporate intelligence, legal or regulated compliance providers.

Do you perform sanctions screening?

We can flag names and risk factors and structure the evidence pack, but formal sanctions clearance and legal interpretation should be completed through the client's compliance function or a qualified specialist using current official lists and tools.

Will you recommend only one partner?

Normally a comparative shortlist is safer. A single-candidate mandate may be appropriate when the client already has a proposed counterparty, but the work then focuses on evidence, gaps and controls rather than selection.
