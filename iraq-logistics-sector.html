Executive summary

The opportunity in brief

The market
Iraq is rebuilding the transport links that connect its Gulf ports, major cities, industrial zones and land borders. The opportunity spans maritime terminals, rail, roads, customs, warehousing, fleet services, cold chain and digital logistics.
The strategic shift
Al Faw Grand Port and the Development Road are intended to create a new north–south trade corridor linking the Gulf to Turkey and Europe. In parallel, Iraq is modernising the existing Umm Qasr–Baghdad–Mosul railway and digitising customs.
The buyers
The Ministry of Transport, General Company for Ports of Iraq, Iraqi Republic Railways, General Company for Land Transport, General Commission for Customs, investment commissions, provincial governments and major private importers.
The core opportunities
Port equipment and operations, rail systems and rolling stock, dry ports, logistics parks, customs technology, tracking, warehousing, cold chain, heavy haulage, fleet management and specialist project cargo.

Market overview

From import gateway to regional corridor

Iraq's logistics market is shaped by a simple reality: most imported goods enter through southern ports or land borders and must then move long distances to Baghdad, the northern governorates and the Kurdistan Region. That creates persistent demand for port capacity, inland transport, warehousing, customs brokerage and reliable freight coordination.

The longer-term opportunity is larger. Al Faw Grand Port is being developed as the southern anchor of the Development Road, a multimodal corridor of more than 1,200 kilometres connecting the Gulf to Turkey. At the same time, a World Bank-backed railway programme is modernising 1,047 kilometres of the existing north–south route between Umm Qasr and Mosul through Baghdad.

Demand drivers

What is pushing the market

Al Faw Grand Port

New port capacity, marine infrastructure and terminal systems create demand across construction, equipment, operations and digital port management.

The Development Road

A planned north–south rail and road corridor designed to connect the Gulf to Turkey and European markets.

Railway modernisation

Rehabilitation of the Umm Qasr–Baghdad–Mosul railway, rolling stock and maintenance capacity.

Customs digitisation

ASYCUDA deployment is changing declaration, revenue and clearance processes at major entry points.

Private consumption and imports

Large urban markets in Baghdad, Basra, Mosul and the Kurdistan Region require dependable inland distribution.

Industrial and project cargo

Energy, construction, water and manufacturing projects need heavy lift, abnormal loads, secure storage and coordinated customs clearance.

Buyers & decision-makers

Who actually awards the work

  • The Ministry of Transport, setting national transport policy and leading major programmes.
  • The General Company for Ports of Iraq, responsible for Iraq's federal commercial ports.
  • Iraqi Republic Railways, the state railway operator and project counterpart.
  • The General Company for Land Transport and other state transport companies.
  • The General Commission for Customs and the Ministry of Finance.
  • The National Investment Commission and provincial investment commissions.
  • Oil, power, construction and industrial project owners requiring specialist logistics.
  • Large importers, distributors, shipping agents and private terminal or warehouse operators.

Market-entry risks

What to plan around

  • Customs classifications, approvals and documentary requirements that can vary by cargo and entry point.
  • Border congestion and inconsistent processing times.
  • Road condition, axle-load restrictions and route surveys for abnormal cargo.
  • Cargo security, seals, tracking and chain-of-custody requirements.
  • Heat, dust and storage conditions, especially for food, pharmaceuticals and sensitive equipment.
  • Different procedures in Federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region.
  • Payment terms and demurrage exposure when clearance or unloading is delayed.
  • Need for reliable local transporters, customs brokers and warehouse operators.

FAQ

Common questions

What are the biggest logistics opportunities in Iraq?

The largest opportunities are linked to Al Faw Grand Port, the Development Road, railway rehabilitation, dry ports and logistics hubs, customs digitisation, warehousing, cold chain, fleet services and specialist freight.

Who awards transport and logistics projects in Iraq?

Key public bodies include the Ministry of Transport, the General Company for Ports of Iraq, Iraqi Republic Railways, the General Company for Land Transport, the General Commission for Customs, the National Investment Commission and provincial authorities.

Can foreign logistics companies operate in Iraq?

Yes. International freight forwarders, shipping lines, terminal operators, EPC contractors, technology suppliers and logistics service companies can operate through local registration, branches, joint ventures, agents or project-specific partnerships.

Is a local partner required?

A local partner is not always a legal requirement, but a vetted Iraqi partner is often valuable for permits, customs, inland transport, tendering, site access and provincial coordination.

What is the Development Road?

The Development Road is Iraq's planned multimodal corridor linking Al Faw Grand Port in the south to Turkey and onward to Europe through rail, road and logistics infrastructure.

What are the main logistics risks?

The main risks are customs and documentation delays, inconsistent procedures across entry points, infrastructure gaps, cargo security, payment terms, border congestion, climate and heat exposure, and differences between Federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region.
