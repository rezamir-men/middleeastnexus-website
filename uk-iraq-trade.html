Why Iraq for UK business

A growing market on Britain's trade map

Total trade between the UK and Iraq is worth around £1 billion a year, and the relationship is deepening fast. In early 2025 the two governments announced a landmark package of British-led projects worth around $15bn — including a UK-led consortium on major water infrastructure in Basra and power-transmission systems for the national grid — more than ten times the previous year's bilateral trade. For UK exporters and contractors in energy, water, infrastructure, healthcare and professional services, Iraq is a large, under-served market that is actively opening to British expertise.

FAQ

Common questions from UK companies

Can a UK company register and operate in Iraq?

Yes. UK companies can establish a local company, register a branch, or work through an Iraqi partner. Investment Law No. 13 offers a licensing route with tax holidays and customs exemptions for qualifying projects. Federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region have separate regimes.

Do UK companies need an Iraqi partner?

A local partner is not always legally required, but a vetted Iraqi partner, agent or subcontractor is often essential in practice for tendering, permits, mobilisation and dealing with local authorities.

How large is UK–Iraq trade?

Total UK–Iraq trade is worth around £1 billion a year, with UK exports of roughly £841 million. In 2025 the two governments announced a package of British-led projects worth around $15bn, including major water infrastructure in Basra.

What is the difference between Federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region?

Federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region operate separate investment laws, company registration processes and tender systems. UK companies should decide early which regime they are entering, as it affects ownership, licensing and contracting.

Which sectors are best for UK companies in Iraq?

Energy and oilfield services, water and infrastructure, power and renewables, healthcare and education, professional and financial services, and manufacturing and equipment — all areas of established British strength matched to strong Iraqi demand.
